<Section address="0x01" title="The study">
  <Tabs

  >
    <TabPanel id="problema">
      <div class="split">
        <p class="statement">
          Reactive allocation only adjusts after it sees demand change. At the
          peaks, it arrives late.
        </p>
        <div class="split-side">
          <p>
            Every few minutes, a cloud provider decides how many instances to
            keep running. Too many is expensive; too few breaks the service
            level agreement (SLA).
          </p>
          <p>
            The alternative is to forecast demand and provision ahead of time.
            This work measures how much that forecast is worth in cost,
            comparing neural networks with traditional methods under the same
            allocation policy.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </TabPanel>
    <TabPanel id="metodo">
      <div class="auto-grid">
        <Card id="M/01" title="Data" flat>
          <p>
            A real production trace from Alibaba (2018), aggregated into CPU
            demand every 5 minutes and split chronologically (60/20/20) so the
            future never leaks into training.
          </p>
        </Card>
        <Card id="M/02" title="Forecasters" flat>
          <p>
            Naive, moving average, seasonal naive, Holt-Winters, ARIMA and a
            PyTorch GRU that forecasts 5 to 30 minutes ahead in a single pass.
          </p>
        </Card>
        <Card id="M/03" title="Fixed policy" flat>
          <p>
            A predictive policy with a safety margin calibrated on validation
            for each violation limit. Only the forecaster changes, so any cost
            difference comes from the forecast.
          </p>
        </Card>
        <Card id="M/04" title="Simulator" flat>
          <p>
            Discrete-time simulation that models instance startup time. It
            measures cost, SLA violations and idle capacity against reactive,
            static and oracle baselines.
          </p>
        </Card>
      </div>
    </TabPanel>
    <TabPanel id="resultado">
      <div class="auto-grid">
        <Stat
          label="GRU · 1% limit"
          value="1.29"
          note="lowest cost that met the limit"
        />
        <Stat
          label="Reactive (HPA) · 1%"
          value="1.34"
          note="and exceeded the limit on test"
        />
        <Stat
          label="Static at peak"
          value="1.83"
          note="never violates, but pays for the slack"
        />
      </div>
      <p class="max-prose">
        Cost normalized by the oracle (1.00 = minimum cost with no violations).
        With a 1% violation limit, the GRU had the lowest cost among the
        configurations that met the limit on the test set. At 5%, the gaps
        between forecasters shrink and ARIMA comes out slightly ahead (1.18).
        The numbers come from a single test window and a single seed, and are
        preliminary.
      </p>
